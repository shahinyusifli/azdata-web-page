Data Catalog vs. Context Layer: They're Not the Same Thing

A data catalog answers "what tables exist, and who owns them." It's built for a human to browse, usually synced on a schedule measured in hours, and governed through a review workflow. That's the right design for its job. It is not the same job as answering an AI agent's actual question, which is closer to: "can I safely and correctly act on this table right now."

That second question needs three things most catalogs don't prioritize: freshness measured in minutes rather than hours, a machine-readable format an agent can consume without a UI in between, and enough business context (not just column names) to avoid a technically-valid, semantically-wrong query. A context layer is what you get when you design for the agent as the primary reader instead of the human.

None of this makes catalogs obsolete. It makes them a different layer, solving a different problem, for a different reader.
